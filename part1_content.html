CELPIP Reading Part 1 practice with answers

Reading Correspondence tests how well you understand a message and its reply. Try this original CELPIP Reading Part 1 practice test as an untimed learning exercise. Read the email and answer six comprehension questions. Then choose words for five blanks in the reply. All 11 answers include explanations. This example is free to read without an account and is separate from our scored practice sets.

Original practice by CELPIP Practice · Reviewed September 18, 2026

How to approach Reading Part 1

  1. Start with the relationship and purpose. Who is writing, who will reply, and what decision or action does the writer need? A polite opening alone does not tell you the purpose.
  2. Track changes carefully. Mark the original arrangement and the new one separately. Words such as instead, still and only often decide which detail answers a question.
  3. Prove each comprehension answer with the message. The correct choice may paraphrase a sentence; an option that repeats a word can still change its meaning.
  4. For the reply, use both messages. Read each completed sentence aloud in your head. Check the facts, the grammar and the writer’s tone before choosing.

Email: a change to the bicycle repair workshop

Subject: Saturday workshop — new meeting point

Hi Maya,

Thanks for offering to help at our bicycle repair workshop this Saturday. I had planned to hold it in the courtyard behind the community centre, but the forecast now shows steady rain. We have booked the centre’s ground-floor activity room instead. The workshop will still run from 10 a.m. to noon. Please arrive at 9:30 so we can lay protective mats under the repair stands before the participants come in.

We have twelve registrations, one for each bicycle workstation in the room. Registration is now closed, so please do not invite additional participants. Two people have asked whether they can bring children. I told them that each registered participant may bring one child, provided the child stays beside them. This exception does not create places for more bicycles. We are keeping the session small so everyone has time to practise.

The centre has a set of basic tools we can borrow, so you do not need to bring your own. However, its pump does not fit every tyre valve. Could you bring the small pump you used at our last event? We will cover punctures and brake checks, but we will not work on electric bicycles because neither of us has training in their electrical systems. Please remind anyone who asks that this is a learning session, not a full repair service.

I have already emailed the participants about the room change. Could you put a sign at the courtyard gate when you arrive? People who miss the email may still head there. Use the side entrance to bring in your bicycle; the main doors will be locked until 10. Let me know by Thursday evening whether you can still help. If the earlier arrival is a problem, I can ask Leo to set up with me.

Thanks again, Priya

Maya’s reply: choose an option for blanks 7–11

Hi Priya,

Thanks for the update. Moving the workshop indoors sounds [7] given the weather forecast. I can still help, but my bus does not arrive until 9:45. Please ask Leo to [8] before I get there.

I will bring my pump, but I will leave my basic tools at home because we can [9]. I will use the side entrance and put up the sign to [10]. My neighbour asked whether you could fix her electric bicycle; I will explain that it is [11].

See you on Saturday, Maya

Questions and answer explanations

Choose an answer before opening its explanation. For blanks, insert the complete option into the message. This learning exercise does not calculate an official CELPIP score.

  1. 1. Why is Priya writing to Maya?

    1. To cancel Saturday’s workshop.
    2. To explain changed arrangements and confirm Maya’s help.
    3. To ask Maya to lead an electrical repair class.
    4. To invite Maya to register as a participant.
    Show answer 1 and explanation

    B — Priya explains the indoor location, setup time and equipment, then asks whether Maya can still help. The event is not cancelled. Maya already offered to volunteer, so this is not a participant invitation. Electrical repairs are explicitly outside the session.

  2. 2. Which part of the original plan stays the same?

    1. The location in the courtyard.
    2. The maximum number of registrations remaining available.
    3. The workshop’s 10 a.m. start.
    4. The use of the main entrance before setup.
    Show answer 2 and explanation

    C — The first paragraph says the workshop will still run from 10 a.m. to noon. “Still” signals an unchanged arrangement. The location moves indoors, registration closes, and volunteers need the side entrance before the main doors open.

  3. 3. What does Priya allow registered participants to do?

    1. Bring one child who stays beside them.
    2. Invite another adult to repair a bicycle.
    3. Register an extra bicycle on arrival.
    4. Leave children at a separate supervised activity.
    Show answer 3 and explanation

    A — The exception permits one child beside each registered participant. It does not reopen registration or add bicycle places. No separate childcare activity is offered. Permission to bring a child does not mean anyone can bring guests.

  4. 4. Why does Priya ask Maya to bring her pump?

    1. The centre has no tools at all.
    2. Maya is expected to give it to the centre permanently.
    3. The pump is needed to repair electrical systems.
    4. The centre’s pump is not suitable for every tyre valve.
    Show answer 4 and explanation

    D — The centre has basic tools, but its pump does not fit every valve. This specific limitation explains the request. Nothing says Maya must donate her pump, and electric bicycles are excluded from the work.

  5. 5. What is the purpose of the sign at the courtyard gate?

    1. To announce that registration has reopened.
    2. To direct participants who have not seen the email to the new room.
    3. To tell volunteers that the workshop starts at 9:30.
    4. To advertise a full bicycle repair service.
    Show answer 5 and explanation

    B — Priya has emailed the room change, but some people may miss it and go to the old meeting point. The sign helps those people find the indoor session. It does not change registration, the 10 a.m. workshop start or the limited learning-service offer.

  6. 6. What does Priya’s offer to ask Leo show?

    1. She no longer wants Maya to attend.
    2. She plans to postpone the workshop if Maya is late.
    3. She can arrange different setup help if Maya cannot arrive early.
    4. She expects Leo to repair electric bicycles.
    Show answer 6 and explanation

    C — Priya offers a backup for setup if the earlier arrival is difficult. This suggests flexibility about volunteer arrangements, not cancellation or rejection of Maya’s help. Nothing connects Leo to electrical repairs.

  7. 7. Choose the best option for blank 7.

    1. sensible
    2. unnecessary
    3. impossible
    4. disappointing because of the sunshine
    Show answer 7 and explanation

    A — “Moving the workshop indoors sounds sensible given the weather forecast” agrees with the practical response to steady rain. “Unnecessary” contradicts that supportive reply. An indoor room is already booked, so “impossible” lacks support, and sunshine contradicts the forecast.

  8. 8. Choose the best option for blank 8.

    1. cancel the registrations
    2. lock the side entrance
    3. change the workshop to 9:45
    4. help with the setup
    Show answer 8 and explanation

    D — Maya cannot arrive at 9:30, so she accepts Priya’s offer of Leo’s setup help. Her bus time does not become the workshop start time. Cancelling registrations or locking the required entrance would not solve the setup problem.

  9. 9. Choose the best option for blank 9.

    1. ask each child to supply them
    2. borrow the centre’s set
    3. buy them from Leo
    4. repair bicycles without any
    Show answer 9 and explanation

    B — Priya says the centre has basic tools they can borrow. That fact makes it reasonable for Maya to leave her own tools at home. The passage does not ask children to supply tools, offer a sale or say that repairs need none.

  10. 10. Choose the best option for blank 10.

    1. invite more people to join
    2. keep all children outside
    3. help people find the new location
    4. tell everyone to return on Thursday
    Show answer 10 and explanation

    C — The sign is for people who go to the courtyard without seeing the email. It redirects them to the activity room. Registration remains closed, permitted children can accompany participants, and Thursday is the reply deadline rather than the event day.

  11. 11. Choose the best option for blank 11.

    1. outside the scope of this workshop
    2. the main topic of the session
    3. welcome if she arrives before 9:30
    4. included as a full repair service
    Show answer 11 and explanation

    A — “Outside the scope” means the request is not covered by this session. Priya explicitly excludes electric bicycles because of the volunteers’ training. Arriving early does not remove that restriction, and the workshop is not a full repair service.

Turn your mistakes into a reading strategy

Sort mistakes into changed details, permissions, purpose and reply context. For example, volunteers are asked to arrive at 9:30. Maya’s bus arrives at 9:45. The workshop starts at 10 a.m. All three times are real details, but only one answers question 2. For each reply blank, point to the fact in Priya’s email that makes the choice fit. Getting 11 out of 11 here does not establish an official CELPIP level. It shows how you handled this one untimed example.

Keep a short error log: your choice, the correct choice, the sentence or table cell that proves it, and the trap you missed. Then try a fresh question set to practise the same skill without relying on memory.
